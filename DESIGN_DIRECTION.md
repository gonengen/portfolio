# Portfolio Redesign — Creative Direction

## Vision

Quiet Confidence.

A refined, mature, thoughtful portfolio for a Senior Product Designer. The work should be the most interesting thing on the page.

## Audience

Design leaders, Heads of Product, hiring managers, founders, and potential clients.

These are people, not applicant tracking systems. Write and design for human understanding.

## Visual Principles

- Understated rather than bold or loud.
- Refined typography, generous but intentional whitespace, and precise alignment.
- Restrained neutral colors that let product screenshots stand out.
- Avoid oversized typography, excessive decoration, aggressive contrast, and unnecessary visual effects.
- Treat actual product screens as the primary visual assets.
- Prioritize composition, image quality, cropping, and presentation context.
- Every detail should feel intentional, including mobile layouts.

## Interaction & Delight

- Delight should be discovered, not announced.
- Use subtle, purposeful microinteractions.
- Favor natural easing and carefully considered timing.
- Every animation should support clarity, feedback, orientation, or personality.
- Avoid unnecessary scroll effects, excessive parallax, and animation for its own sake.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Content & Copywriting

- Write for humans, not ATS systems or SEO keyword stuffing.
- Use plain, conversational, confident English.
- Explain what the reader is looking at and why it matters.
- Prefer concrete observations and decisions over abstract design terminology.
- Avoid corporate buzzwords and inflated claims.
- Keep copy concise without sacrificing context.
- Show reasoning, tradeoffs, constraints, and outcomes.
- Never invent research findings, metrics, or business impact.
- Assume the reader is intelligent but unfamiliar with the product.

## Experience Principles

- Make the portfolio easy to scan without making it superficial.
- Let visitors understand each project's context quickly.
- Create a clear path from the problem to the design decisions and resulting experience.
- Give screenshots enough room to be examined.
- Use captions and annotations where they genuinely improve comprehension.
- Balance storytelling with the ability to jump directly to the work.

## Technical Direction

- Work code-first using React, Vite, and the existing repository.
- Figma is optional inspiration, not a source of truth.
- Preserve existing content and URLs unless changes are explicitly approved.
- Build responsive behavior intentionally.
- Maintain accessibility, performance, and semantic structure.
- Work only on `portfolio-redesign`.
- Never push to `main` or deploy to production without explicit authorization.

## Design Review Questions

Before approving a change, ask:

1. Does it make the work easier to understand?
2. Does it feel refined rather than decorated?
3. Is the interaction purposeful?
4. Does the copy sound like a thoughtful human explaining their work?
5. Would removing this element make the experience better?
